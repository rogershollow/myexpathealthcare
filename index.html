The opportunity

Finding trustworthy care abroad is hard. That's the business.

When you relocate, few things matter more — or are harder to sort out — than healthcare you can trust. Which doctors speak English? Who genuinely understands expat patients, insurance, and the small anxieties of being new? My Expat Healthcare answers that with a vetted, curated guide, supported by helpful content and a personal advisory touch.

Panama is an ideal first market. It's one of the world's most established retiree destinations, private care in the main hubs is delivered by English-speaking, often U.S.-trained doctors, and internationally affiliated hospitals are the norm. The audience already exists and is actively looking — MEH's job is to surface and vouch for the expat-friendly providers among them.

Contact Taxonomy Proposal

02

Where this lives in Zoho

The SOW names Zoho Contacts, but the standalone Zoho Contacts app is a simple address book: no custom fields, no tags, no saved views. The taxonomy below needs those.

RecommendationBuild in the Zoho CRM Contacts module if the account has CRM (the SOW's social scope references Zoho CRM, so it likely does). CRM gives us custom fields, tags, saved views, mass update, and built-in dedupe — everything this plan uses.
FallbackIf only the Contacts app is available, the taxonomy compresses into default fields + groups (types become groups, extras go into Notes). Workable, but capped. Confirm on day 1 of access.
03

05

Tags — why each works, and how they stack

Rule of thumb: fields hold single-truth, reportable attributes; tags hold cross-cutting flags that can stack. In Zoho CRM, tags are additive labels on a record, and saved views can combine tag + field criteria (e.g., tag AND type AND hub) — that combination is what makes stacking useful. Tags are only created deliberately; a sprawling tag list is how databases rot.

Why each starting tag earns its place:

Interview Guest

Cuts across every type. Guests can be Providers, Expat Leads, or Partners — so it can't be a Contact Type or a type-specific field. As a tag, one click gives the full guest history: who's been on the show, who's booked, who to thank and follow up. It's the bridge between the contact base and the content engine.

Newsletter

Intent, separated from permission. The tag marks who wants the newsletter; the Marketing Consent field marks who legally may be emailed. The actual send list is the saved view "tag = Newsletter AND Consent = Yes." If consent is withdrawn, the interest signal isn't lost — flip the field, keep the tag.

VIP

A priority lens, not a category. A flagship provider, a high-value referral partner, an influential expat — VIPs exist in every type. The tag drives service level (faster turnaround in ongoing support, first pick for interviews) without ever distorting the type-based reporting.

Follow-up

A to-do queue with zero extra modules. Filtering by this tag turns the contact base into today's action list — no pipeline or task system built (that's future scope). Do the thing, remove the tag. It gives the monthly 5 support hours a working queue for free.

Needs Info

Data quality made visible. Applied during cleanup and at entry to anything below the minimum-viable-contact standard. The monthly hygiene pass is literally "open the Needs Info view and chase." And the count is a health metric — trending down means the database is getting better.

06

Data-entry conventions

  1. One record per person. Clinics/companies live in the Company field (or Accounts, if CRM) — never as a "person" contact.
  2. Search before create. Check email first; no blind new records.
  3. Minimum viable contact at creation: Name + (Email or Phone) + Contact Type + Source. No exceptions — a contact without these is a Needs Info from birth.
  4. Names Proper Case, emails lowercase, phones E.164.
  5. Changes are edits to the existing record (never a duplicate), with a dated line in Notes for anything material.

04

Expert notes — the choices that matter

1
Inquiry Response is the highest-stakes templateSpeed and warmth on the first reply is where trust is won or lost — it gets the most copy attention, and pairs with the 1-hour TAT.
2
One job, one CTA per emailEvery email carries exactly one call to action. Multi-ask emails depress response — the follow-up exists so the second ask has its own send.
3
The Newsletter Shell is one layout, many issuesLocked header/footer, swappable story blocks — each issue becomes content work, not design work.
4
Thank-you / Referral is the growth templateReferrals are how a trust business grows; asking at the moment of delivered value is when yes-rates peak.
5
Compliance is built into the footerEvery email ships with an unsubscribe line wired to Marketing Consent, and sends always respect Do Not Contact — matching the SOP's same-day consent rule.
6
Mobile-first, skimmableShort paragraphs, generous type — the audience skews retiree, so legibility beats cleverness.
7
Merge fields = taxonomy fieldsEvery bracketed field is a field in the approved taxonomy — personalization comes free from clean data, with no manual editing.
